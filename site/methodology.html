1. What is recorded

Each price is one row in a public data file. Every row records:

  • Material and unit, from a fixed list of material names, so the same material is always named the same way.
  • Spec: brand, grade or size. If the source does not say, the row says so.
  • Region the price applies to, and the month it applies to.
  • Delivery basis: ex-depot, delivered, or not stated.
  • Source: who published or quoted the price, and a link to the page where possible.
  • Date recorded: the day the price was checked and entered.

Prices are recorded as the source gives them. They are not adjusted for VAT, transport, quantity discounts or payment terms unless the row says so.

2. Where prices come from

  • Published reports: articles or reports that state a price, linked to the page.
  • Supplier price lists: prices a supplier publishes, linked to the page.
  • Supplier quotes: a named supplier's price given by phone, WhatsApp or in person. Many suppliers do not publish prices online, so these rows can have no public link. They are marked "no public link" everywhere they appear.

When sources disagree, both prices are kept with their own sources. Nothing is averaged by hand.

3. How data is checked

  • A person checks every price. Each row is entered only after the source has been checked by hand. No price on this site was made up, estimated or generated by software.
  • An automatic validator checks the format of every row before the site is built: required fields, valid months and dates, whole-naira prices, valid links, known material names, no duplicates. It checks format, not truth.
  • Big jumps are flagged for a second look. A move of more than 20% between two recorded months raises a warning. It never blocks the price, because Nigerian prices genuinely move fast.
  • Every change is version-controlled. The data file's history records when each price was added or changed.

4. Calculations

  • Latest price: the most recent month with a price for that material and region.
  • Several sources in one month: the median (middle value) is shown, with the number of sources used. With one source, that single price is shown.
  • Change: latest month compared with the previous month that has a price. One month apart is labelled "month-on-month". Otherwise it says the real gap, for example "over 2 months", because calling a two-month change month-on-month would overstate how fast prices move. With one month of data it shows "n/a".
  • Gaps on the chart: a dashed line means no price was recorded for the months in between. Missing months are never filled in.
  • Basket pricer line total: quantity × latest price, rounded to the nearest naira.
  • Escalation estimate (an estimate, not a forecast). Only shown for a material with at least 3 months of prices:
    1. For each pair of consecutive recorded months: (Pnow ÷ Pthen)1 ÷ months apart − 1, a monthly-equivalent rate.
    2. Average those rates over up to the last 6 recorded months to get g.
    3. Allowance for m months until purchase: (1 + g)m − 1.
    It assumes recent average movement continues. It does not predict shocks, policy changes or exchange-rate moves.
  • Basket index: not published yet. It needs a fixed, weighted basket of materials with regular prices and a base month set to 100. It will be added once enough materials have regular data.

5. Limits

  • Coverage is small and growing. A material or region with one or two prices tells you little about a trend.
  • A region label can cover a wide area. Prices inside a city can vary by location and supplier.
  • Chart lines group all specs of a material together. Check the spec of each source before comparing.
  • Prices may not match what you are quoted for your quantity, location, delivery terms or payment terms.
  • This site provides evidence for your own judgement. It is not professional cost advice.

6. Found an error?

If you see a price that looks wrong, please tell us the material, region and month, and what you believe is correct, with a source if you have one.
